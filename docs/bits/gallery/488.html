Gehitri


Oh. my. code. I love drawing during the roleplaying game session.

20261113 1833


I was dreaming to join a tabletop roleplaying match for several years. I was building up expectations, and this session met them perfectly. It was exciting, and I was seeing images of different situations, which I had been putting on paper immediately, flawlessly meeting the feeling.

A technician, a navigator and a biologist went on a mission to obtain biological material of an ancient, long-dead beast on the frozen planet Gehitri.
Fully prepared for an exploration, they safely landed in a vast snow desert amidst ragged icebound peaks. They proceeded on a speeder to the canyon where they knew the beast lay.
As they stood upon a cliff, they knew they were not alone. Native tiger-lions of this harsh world kept watch on the intruders from afar as they approached the rift.
Even with vital equipment researchers felt cold. More so disturbing, however, was what they heard. A howl, or a hum was coming seemingly from everywhere, and then suddenly cut off. And then started again.
Harbouring their unrest, the explorers descended to see the beast close. They witnessed it frozen solid in the canyon wall of ice. They were preparing to take the samples.
Lee Cooper suddenly spoke, in fear and amazement. He said he heard the beast talk. It had not moved, imprisoned in frost. And yet Lee insisted that he heard it talk, begging to be released.
A sure leader, Mirian Cooper had to take action. With voice of reason and wits, she drove her point that the creature was dead as any could be. Lee could not disagree more. He enabled his scanner to seek brain activity, and, some minutes later, exclaimed, showing positive readings to his unamused comrades.
After a long dispute the team arrived at a compromise: they will extract as little material as they possibly can to alleviate any harm to the magnificent creature.
Aided by Wolverton, Lee diligently drilled the brittle ice and gathered merciful minimum of the ancient body.
Everything went silent. Then came the noise. It was louder than any before. Howling was coming from all directions, but amidst the cacophony moaning clearly formed into words of primal anger of the beast.
Still dazzled by the whispers, Lee Cooper could not abandon the elder creature in anguish. He decided to sing from the bottom of his heart, and Mirian choose to assist. Their singing was so very horrid and outrageous, that the voice around them boomed with newfound might with a plea to end the unbearable chant.
Consequently, in sure unison, all three scientists hurried back to their hovercraft, facing the threat of tiger-lions, which surrounded the frozen plateau and approached the vessel.
Lee Cooper readied his sniper rifle with a steady hand, justifying to jesting Mirian his violence against the fauna by presenting the tranquilliser darts. In the meantime Wolverton rushed to the cockpit, firing up the engines and leading the hovercraft back to the spaceship at the terminal velocity.
The explorers managed to return with biological samples unscathed but not unshaken. Their contractor was disappointed with the poor amounts of research materials, and left immediately after rather unwillingly paying the team.
Wolverton, Lee Cooper and Mirian Cooper were therefore left with their questions unanswered, having to do nothing but guess what secrets they left behind along with the mysterious creature on the frigid world of Gehitri.
